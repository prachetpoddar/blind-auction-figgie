Rules

The deck

Forty cards. One suit has 12, one has 8 and two have 10. The goal suit is the other suit of the same colour as the 12-card suit.

The pot

Everyone antes 50 of their 350 chips, so the pot holds 200 and you start with 300.

The data desk

Before each trading window the data desk opens. It sells four widgets, each in its own sealed auction:

  • Peek: one random card from a random opponent, never one you have already seen. Two lots each desk.
  • Scan: five random cards from the opponent you pick. One lot.
  • X-ray: the whole hand of the opponent you pick, kept current as that player trades. Two lots.
  • Tracker: a live grid of every player's buys and sells for the rest of the round. Bots never buy it, so it sells at the house price.

Each lot goes to the highest sealed bids. Every winner pays the clearing price: the best losing bid, or the house reserve if that is higher. You never pay more than you bid. All payments go to the house, not the pot.

What others see

After each desk everyone sees who bought which widget, whose hand it was aimed at, and the price. Nobody sees the cards except the buyer. A bot may tell the table what it peeked, truthfully or not.

Trading

Each window lasts 60 seconds. Sell into the best bid, buy from the best offer, or post your own prices. One card changes hands per trade. Every trade, by anyone, cancels all of your resting quotes, so post again after it. The arrow beside a best price pennies it: one tick (0.1) better, as your own quote. You can also trade from the keyboard; see Helper keys.

Modes

Standard gives four 60-second windows, 4 minutes in all. Learning gives four 5-minute windows, 20 minutes in all, with the bots' hands either shown face up or hidden. The rules are otherwise the same.

Payout

After four desks and four windows, each goal-suit card pays 10. Whoever holds the most goal-suit cards takes the rest of the pot.

The bots

Each bot plays one of five styles. You learn which when the round ends.

Example payouts

Say Hearts had 12 cards, so Diamonds is the goal suit, and Diamonds has 10 cards. Each Diamond pays 10, which takes 100 of the pot, and the 100 left goes to whoever holds the most Diamonds. If you end with 4 Diamonds and no one has more, you collect 40 plus 100. If you and Ines both hold 4, you each collect 40 plus 50.

If the goal suit has 8 cards, they pay 80 together and the bonus is 120.

Basic strategy

Your own hand is evidence. Holding many of one suit makes it more likely that suit is the 12-card suit, and so more likely that its same-colour partner is the goal suit.

Watch who buys. A player who keeps buying one suit may know something. Someone who just bought data and then trades hard is telling you something.

The bonus is worth more than any one card. Being the single largest holder of the goal suit matters more than the price of the last card you need.

Data is cheap evidence, not an answer. One card shown is worth under a chip on its own; a whole hand is worth more, mostly for knowing who is racing you for the bonus. Buy what would change your next trade.

Rematch the same bots to learn their styles over several rounds; the standings track who is ahead.
